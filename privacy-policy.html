Privacy Policy for Canter

Last Updated: September 25, 2026

Overview

Canter, formerly RunWalk ("the App"), is a run-walk interval timer available for iOS, watchOS, and Android. Your privacy is important to us, and we want to be transparent about our data practices.

In short: The App never sends us, the developer, your workouts, routes, GPS location, health data or Strava account details, and from your settings it sends only the new on or off value of a main switch you change. What it does send, on both iOS and Android, is anonymous usage events and crash reports to PostHog, a product analytics service: which screens are opened, when a workout starts and ends with its duration and distance as plain numbers, and the technical details of a crash, tied to a random identifier created for the installation, not to you. You can turn this off in the App under Settings > Privacy > Share anonymous usage and crash reports; what the reports carry is described under Usage and crash reports. The iOS app also asks Firebase App Check to confirm that it is a genuine copy, and the Google Maps SDK inside the Android app sends Google its own diagnostics and usage measurements, both described below. Your workouts live on your device and, if you use the platform's own features, in your own iCloud account or Google account. Apart from PostHog, the only places the App itself sends anything are Apple and Google, through the map, health, backup, sync, app check and rating features described below; what your health store or your account does with its copy afterwards (other apps you have allowed, an export you set up) is described below too. Beyond that, the only things that can reach us are what you choose to send: an email to support, answers to an optional feedback survey in the App, feedback you enter while the Android app is in a closed test on Google Play, or a review you enter in a store, which the store publishes and also shows us in its developer console under the name you review with.

Data the App Handles

Location Data

The App uses your device's GPS location only while a workout is running to:

  • Track your running/walking route
  • Calculate distance and pace
  • Display your route on a map
Important:
  • GPS tracking can be switched off in Settings; a distance-based workout needs it on
  • Your route is stored in your workout history on your device, and travels with that history only as described under Where Your Data Lives (your own iCloud sync on iOS, your own Google backup on Android)
  • The App never sends your GPS location to us. While a route map is on screen, the map tiles are requested from Apple Maps or Google Maps, and those requests carry the map area being displayed, which is where you are or were, together with your IP address (see Third-Party Services)
  • Usage and crash reports carry no GPS position. PostHog does estimate an approximate location from the IP address of each connection, as described under Usage and crash reports
  • Location is read only while the App is tracking a workout, never in the background otherwise

Health & Fitness Data

On iOS and watchOS (Apple HealthKit)

With your explicit permission, the App may:

  • Write: Workout sessions, duration, distance, active calories burned
  • Read: The workouts Canter itself wrote to Apple Health, to keep the app's history in step with them

On Android (Health Connect)

With your explicit permission, the App may:

  • Write: Exercise sessions, distance, active calories burned
  • Read: Nothing. The Android app writes only and never reads from Health Connect
Important:
  • Health data written by the App lives in Apple Health or Health Connect on your device, under their security and your own health-sharing settings (Apple Health may also sync through your iCloud account if you have that enabled)
  • The App never sends health data to us. Usage events carry a finished workout's duration and distance as plain numbers, the same figures the App shows you, but never calories, heart rate, your route or anything read from your health store (see Usage and crash reports). The App itself writes workout duration, distance and calories only to your health store when you allow it, and its history is copied only to the places described under Where Your Data Lives: the sync or backup copy kept under your own account, and a new device you transfer to directly. What happens to the health store afterwards is under your control there: other apps you have allowed can read it, and Health Connect can export it to a cloud provider you choose (Android 14 and later). Those copies are managed, and deleted, in the health store or at that provider, not in the App
  • Other apps you have allowed in Apple Health or Health Connect can read the workouts Canter wrote there; that sharing is yours to grant and revoke in the health store
  • Health data is never used for advertising or marketing

Data the App Does NOT Collect

The App:

  • Does NOT ask for your name, email address or any other account details; using the App needs no identity at all
  • Does NOT require an account or sign-in
  • Does NOT include any advertising SDK and does NOT use your device's advertising identifier. What measurement there is: the App sends anonymous usage events and crash reports to PostHog, as described under Usage and crash reports, which you can turn off in Settings; the Google Maps SDK on Android measures its own use for Google; and Apple and Google may keep their own operational records of their sync, backup, health and Firebase services under their policies
  • Does NOT contain advertisements
  • Does NOT send your workouts, routes, GPS location, health data or Strava account details to us, and sends nothing from your settings except the new on or off value of a main switch you change. Usage events carry only what is listed under Usage and crash reports: screen names, coarse numbers such as a workout's duration and distance, the identifier of a built-in preset or training plan, and the new on or off value of a main switch you change in Settings. Apart from those usage events and crash reports, nothing the App does on its own reaches the developer; only what you choose to send us (a support email, survey answers, closed-test feedback, or a store review) does
  • Does NOT access your contacts, photos, files or messages

Where Your Data Lives

The App stores on your device:

  • Workout history - Your completed workouts, with their routes when GPS was on
  • App settings - Your preferences (voice, sounds, GPS mode, units)
  • Training plan progress - Which plan you picked and which sessions you completed

On iOS, your workout history also syncs between your own devices through your iCloud account (Apple CloudKit), if iCloud is enabled for the App. Apple stores that copy under your Apple Account, protected by Apple's iCloud data protection under your own account settings, and handles it under Apple's privacy policy. Separately, the App's local history and settings are eligible for your device's own backups (iCloud Backup, or a backup to a computer), because the App does not exclude its data from them; whether a copy exists depends on your backup settings, including whether Canter is switched on within them. A restore from a completed backup brings back the history it contained.

On Android, your workout history, settings and plan progress are eligible for Android's app backup when you have Google backup enabled, within Android's limits on backup size, and in a direct device-to-device transfer when you choose one during setup, which works whether or not cloud backup is on. Google stores the backup under your Google Account, encrypted in transit and at rest; on supported Android versions with a PIN, pattern or password set, it is additionally encrypted with your lock-screen secret. Google handles it under Google's privacy policy. A reinstall with backup on can restore what a completed backup contained.

We have no access to your device, your iCloud account or your Google account, so we cannot see any of these copies.

Third-Party Services

The App uses these platform services, each under its own privacy policy:

  • Apple HealthKit and Apple Health (iOS/watchOS) - Apple Privacy Policy
  • Apple iCloud (CloudKit) (iOS/watchOS) - history sync between your own devices - Apple Privacy Policy
  • Apple Maps (iOS/watchOS) - route maps - Apple Privacy Policy
  • Google Health Connect (Android) - Google Privacy Policy
  • Google Maps SDK for Android (Android) - route maps - Google Privacy Policy
  • Android Backup (Android) - your own Google Account backup - Google Privacy Policy
  • PostHog (iOS and Android) - anonymous usage events, crash reports and optional feedback survey answers - PostHog Privacy Policy
  • Firebase App Check (iOS) - proof that requests come from the genuine app - Firebase Privacy and Security
  • App Store and Google Play rating prompts - the rating sheet you may see after a few workouts is shown by Apple's or Google's store. A rating or review you enter there is collected by that store and published the way store reviews are; while the Android app is in a closed test on Google Play, that feedback goes to the developer instead of being published

Map tiles. While a route map is on screen, the tiles are requested from Apple Maps (iOS) or Google Maps (Android). Those requests carry the map area being displayed and your device's IP address, and are handled under Apple's or Google's privacy policy.

Google Maps SDK. On Android, the Maps SDK that draws the map also collects, for Google, technical information about its use: device model and operating system version, the SDK version, a pseudonymous identifier for the SDK installation, diagnostics if the SDK crashes, and events when the map is panned or zoomed. Google uses this to measure the use of and improve its services, as described in its Maps SDK data disclosure. The App creates the map only when a route is shown.

Usage and crash reports (iOS and Android)

The iPhone and Android apps send anonymous usage events and crash reports to PostHog, Inc., a product analytics service, which holds them for us in its United States cloud, where we can read them. The Apple Watch app sends none of its own; a workout started on the watch can be reported by the iPhone app, marked as started on the watch.

Usage events. A usage event records that something happened in the App: the App was installed, updated, opened or sent to the background; a screen was shown, by the screen's name; a workout started, was saved or was discarded; a training plan was started, a plan workout was saved or a plan was completed; Strava was connected, or an upload to Strava succeeded or failed; the store's rating sheet was requested; or one of the main switches in Settings (bells, haptics, voice, GPS, saving to Health, auto-pause, reminders, starting with a walk, and this reporting switch) was turned on or off. On Android, when the App is opened through a Canter link, as the home-screen widget's quick start does, the event also carries that link, which holds at most a workout's run and walk interval lengths, and, when the opening app supplies one, the referrer that names it. Workout events carry coarse facts: the workout mode, the identifier of the built-in preset or plan day (or "custom"), the plan week and day, whether GPS was on, where the workout was started (phone, watch, widget, shortcut or quick start), and, when it ends, its duration in seconds, its distance in whole meters, the number of intervals, whether auto-pause was used, and, for a discarded workout, why it was discarded. They never carry your route, your pace over time, your GPS position or anything from your health store, and the Strava events never carry your Strava name, account or activities.

Details on every report. Every usage event and crash report also carries the App's name, version and build, the device's manufacturer and model, the operating system and its version, the screen size, the language and region setting, the time zone, the type of network connection (for example Wi-Fi or cellular), whether the App is a test build or runs on a simulator or emulator, a session identifier, and a random identifier for the installation. On Android it also carries the name of the mobile network operator, when the phone reports one, and the system's standard user-agent string, which repeats the Android version and device model. It never carries your device's name, your phone number, your Apple Account or Google Account, or your device's advertising identifier.

Crash reports. A crash report is sent when the App crashes or runs into an error we have chosen to record, such as the iCloud sync store failing to open on iOS. It carries the kind of error, its message and the stack trace (the list of the App's functions that were running at the time), together with the details above.

The installation identifier. The random identifier is created by PostHog's library inside the App the first time it runs with reporting on. It is not taken from your device, your Apple Account or your Google Account. It lives in the App's own storage on the device: uninstalling the App removes it, and a reinstall starts a new one. On iOS it is part of the App data that your device's own backups contain, so restoring such a backup brings it back; on Android it is left out of the App's backup. The App never tells PostHog who you are, so PostHog keeps no profile of you, only events under that identifier.

IP address and approximate location. Every connection to PostHog, like any internet connection, gives PostHog your device's IP address. Our PostHog project is set to discard client IP addresses, so the address is not stored with the reports. Before discarding it, PostHog uses it to estimate an approximate location, which is stored with each report: the country and continent and, where the address allows, the region, city and postal code, approximate coordinates of that area with their accuracy radius, and a time zone. This estimate describes where your internet connection appears to be. It comes from the IP address, never from GPS.

How long they are kept. PostHog keeps these events for the retention period of our PostHog plan: one year on PostHog's free plan and seven years on its paid plans, as PostHog's events retention page sets out. PostHog handles them under its privacy policy.

Turning them off. You can turn usage events and crash reports off in the App under Settings > Privacy > Share anonymous usage and crash reports. The switch is on when you install the App. Turning it off sends one last event recording that the switch was turned off, then stops all usage events and crash reports at once; while it stays off, the App does not start PostHog's library at all. Turning it back on resumes them. Reports already sent carry no name or account, only the random installation identifier, which the App never shows you, so nothing links them to you: we cannot find the reports that came from your device, and so cannot locate them for deletion. They leave PostHog at the end of the retention period. Email support if you have questions about them.

Feedback survey. The App may show a short, optional survey inside the App, asking what would make Canter better for you and how likely you are to keep using it. Answering is up to you, and you can dismiss it. Your answers are sent to PostHog with the same random installation identifier as the usage events, and we read the written answers ourselves. Please do not put your name, email address, health details or other personal details in them. The survey is shown only while usage and crash reports are switched on, and answers already sent are kept and handled like the reports above.

Earlier versions. Versions of the iOS app that do not have the Share anonymous usage and crash reports switch send crash reports, and short session reports from which crash-free sessions are counted, to Google's Firebase Crashlytics instead of PostHog, as the previous version of this policy described. Those reports carry the crash trace, device details and pseudonymous identifiers that Firebase assigns, and Google begins removing crash traces after 90 days. Versions of the Android app without that switch send no usage events or crash reports.

Firebase App Check (iOS). Firebase App Check, on iOS, asks Apple's App Attest service to vouch that the request comes from a genuine copy of the App and passes that proof to Firebase; the exchange carries the app's identity, device and operating-system details, a key identifier and Apple's attestation object, but none of your workout, route or health content. Every connection the iOS app makes to Firebase for an app check also gives Google your device's IP address and the usual connection details, which Google uses to operate, secure and improve Firebase under its own privacy policy.

Beyond these platform services and PostHog, the App sends nothing to any advertising network, other analytics platform or other third-party data collection service.

Managing Your Data

Location Permissions

  • iOS: Settings > Privacy & Security > Location Services > Canter
  • Android: Settings > Apps > Canter > Permissions > Location

Health Permissions

  • iOS 18 and later: Settings > Apps > Health > Data Access & Devices > Canter (earlier iOS: Settings > Health > Data Access & Devices > Canter), or in the Health app under Sharing > Apps and Services
  • Android 14 and later: Settings > Security & privacy > Privacy controls > Health Connect > App permissions > Canter (earlier Android: open the Health Connect app > App permissions > Canter)

Usage and Crash Reports

  • iOS and Android: In the App, Settings > Privacy > Share anonymous usage and crash reports

Deleting Your Data

  • Workout history in the App: Delete a workout in the App. On iOS the deletion syncs to your other devices through iCloud
  • Health records: A workout written to Apple Health or Health Connect is a separate copy; delete it in the Health app or in Health Connect. Deleting it there does not remove it from the App's history, and deleting it in the App does not remove it from your health store
  • iCloud copy (iOS): Deleting a workout in the App removes the synced copy once sync has run. Turning off iCloud for Canter stops future sync but does not delete what is already stored; remove that in Settings > Apple Account > iCloud > Manage Account Storage > Canter
  • Device backups (iOS): Your device's own backups are separate from the iCloud sync copy; deleting a synced workout does not touch them. To remove the App's data from iCloud Backup, go to Settings > Apple Account > iCloud > Manage Account Storage > Backups, choose the device, and turn Canter off; that deletes the App's backed-up data and keeps it out of future backups, while the rest of the device's backup stays. Deleting the whole device backup there removes every app's data, not only Canter's. A backup on a computer is deleted on that computer
  • Google backup (Android): The backup copy follows Google's backup settings on your device (Settings > Google > Backup). Those controls are device-wide: turning app backup off and deleting the backup removes the backup of every app on the device, not only the App's. A reinstall with backup on may restore your history
  • Usage and crash reports, and survey answers: Turning the switch off stops new reports at once. Reports and answers already sent carry only a random installation identifier that nothing links to you, so we cannot locate them for deletion; they leave PostHog at the end of the retention period described under Usage and crash reports
  • Uninstalling: Removes the App's own storage on the device: your workout history, settings, plan progress and the random installation identifier used for usage and crash reports. On iOS, the App Check token and attestation record that Firebase keeps in the device keychain may survive an uninstall, as keychain entries do. It does not by itself remove your health records, your iCloud copy or your Google backup
  • What you sent us: A support email stays in our support mailbox so we can answer follow-ups; write to the same address and we will delete it. Feedback entered during a closed test on Google Play is shown to us in the Google Play Console and kept there under Google's rules; we do not copy it anywhere else
